Your AI agent's vision bill is probably the most expensive line item in the whole pipeline, and almost nobody's budgeting for it.

One 2026 benchmark found vision-capable agents burning roughly 45x more tokens than text-only, API-calling agents. Why? Because every screenshot or frame costs thousands of input tokens before the model even starts reasoning. You're paying full frontier-model prices just to look.

And here's the part that should really sting: two separate benchmarks this year (Apple's MM-ToolSandBox, AgentVista) found that the thing actually limiting multimodal agents isn't reasoning or tool selection. It's bad eyes — the best model on AgentVista's hardest visual tasks hit 27.3% accuracy. You're overpaying for perception that still doesn't work.

The fix isn't a bigger VLM. It's an older idea wearing a new hat:

→ Put a small, cheap, purpose-built detector in front of the expensive model.
→ Let it answer "is there even anything here worth looking at" for a fraction of a cent.
→ Only escalate the frames that actually need judgment to the agent that costs real money.

Roboflow quietly shipped a tutorial on exactly this pattern a couple weeks ago. It's not a hack — it's just good systems design applied to vision, the same way you wouldn't run a full LLM call to check if a request is malformed JSON.

This is the layer we live in: specialist detection and segmentation models, tuned and deployed cheap, sitting in front of (or instead of) the expensive general model. If your agent's vision costs are climbing faster than its actual usefulness, that's a pipeline architecture problem — not a "wait for the next model" problem.
