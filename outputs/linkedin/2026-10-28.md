Everyone's demo reel this year has a robot that can "see a cup and pick up the cup."

Nobody's demo reel shows the 280ms of dead air between "sees the cup" and "picks up the cup."

That gap is the whole ballgame with vision-language-action models right now. VLA is the trend everyone's narrating — perception feeding straight into decision and motor commands, no separate planner bolted on after the fact. Very exciting in a paper. Very different animal once you have to close that loop in real time on hardware that fits inside a robot instead of a GPU cluster.

Here's what the papers don't tell you:

→ Your language model doesn't need to run on the same chip as your action head. It usually shouldn't.
→ "Real-time" for a VLA pipeline means your perception stack needs a latency budget measured in milliseconds, not "fast enough for the demo."
→ The failure mode isn't the model being wrong. It's the model being right 40ms too late.

This is exactly the kind of perception-to-edge engineering problem we spend our days in — TensorRT, Jetson-class hardware, CUDA kernels tuned until the bottleneck moves somewhere you can actually live with.

If you're staring down a VLA or embodied-AI pipeline and the latency math isn't adding up yet, that's a conversation worth having before you're debugging it in the field.
